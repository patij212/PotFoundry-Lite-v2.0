# All-20 sweep + STL/render export (2026-06-30)

The in-house kernel run across all 20 styles: isotropic surface metric vs crease-aligned anisotropic metric
(triangle budget + chord fidelity), with a binary STL + render dump per style. Lab-only; the meshes are the
OUTER-WALL SURFACE (high-quality tessellation, not yet the closed watertight solid — seam + rim/base deferred).
Runner: `research/bridge/allTwentyExport.test.ts` (PF_ALL20). Data: `2026-06-30-rebaseline-evidence/all20-sweep.json`.
STLs: `research/exchange/_all20/stl/` (20 files, 459 MB). Montage render: `scratchpad/all20_montage.png`.

## Result (tol 0.012, iso = robust default; crease = efficiency variant)
**Isotropic metric is CAD-grade across ALL 20** — mean min-angle **45.5°**, median %<20° **0.6%**. Chord rms is
sub-printer on the smooth/moderate styles (0.003–0.04) and higher only on the steep-relief styles
(ArtDeco 0.37, BasketWeave 0.13, BambooSegments 0.07, DragonScales 0.058) — those close with more density (the
in-house kernel already hit rms 0.003 on Gyroid at 3M).

**Crease-aligned wins the triangle budget on ~6 styles** (≈2.5–8× fewer triangles at acceptable chord):
ArtDeco, DragonScales, BambooSegments, BasketWeave, CelticTriquetra, LowPolyFacet (the extended-feature /
stepped / facet styles). Iso wins on the other 14 (fine-everywhere or near-C0 styles, where anisotropy is
ill-conditioned or doesn't help).

| metric | winners | quality |
|---|---|---|
| iso (robust default) | 14/20 | mean 45.5°, median %<20° 0.6%, CAD-grade everywhere |
| crease-aligned | 6/20 | 2.5–8× fewer tris on extended-feature styles |

## Per-style metric policy (recommendation)
- **Default = isotropic surface metric** — CAD-grade on every style; the safe production choice.
- **Crease-aligned = opt-in for triangle budget** on extended-feature styles (ripples/flutes/ridges/steps/
  facets) when triangle count matters (LOD, file size), judged by chord + in-metric angle (not iso angle).
- Steep near-C0 styles (Gyroid, BasketWeave creases) → iso + density; anisotropy is the wrong tool there.

## Deliverables
20 binary STLs (per-face normals, outer-wall surface) + 20 render dumps + the montage. STL is the in-house
kernel's actual mesh output — open in a slicer/viewer to inspect the tessellation. To become printable solids
they need the seam weld + inner wall + rim + base (the "full pot" assembly, next).
